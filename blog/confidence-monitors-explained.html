Confidence Monitors: What They Are and When to Use Them

Professional Teleprompter Guide

If you've been researching teleprompter setups for your Georgia event, you've likely come across the term "confidence monitor." But what exactly is a confidence monitor, and does your event need one?

This guide from Georgia Teleprompter draws on 25+ years of professional experience serving clients including Fortune 500 companies, Atlanta film productions, political campaigns, and hundreds of corporate events across Georgia—from Atlanta keynotes to Savannah galas to Augusta conferences.

What Is a Confidence Monitor?

A confidence monitor is a display screen placed in front of or below the speaker, showing them their upcoming script or talking points. Unlike traditional teleprompters that use reflective glass to display text invisibly to the audience, confidence monitors are visible to everyone in the room.

Think of it as a "teleprompter for the speaker only." The speaker can glance down at the text while maintaining a natural posture, without looking like they're reading from a script.

Confidence monitors serve several purposes:

  • Displaying the speaker's script or talking points
  • Showing timing and pacing cues
  • Providing cues from stage managers or directors
  • Displaying audience questions during Q&A sessions
  • Showing guest names and introductions

Types of Confidence Monitor Setups

Floor Monitors

The most common setup for live events. A monitor sits on the floor in front of the stage, angled so the speaker can look down slightly to read while maintaining eye contact with the audience. These work well for stages of any size and are the standard choice for corporate events at the Georgia World Congress Center, Cobb Galleria, and Atlanta hotel conference rooms.

Desktop Monitors

Integrated Stage Displays

Larger LED displays built into stage set designs. These are common at major events, award shows, and product launches in Atlanta's entertainment venues like the Fox Theatre and Mercedes-Benz Stadium.

Wireless Confidence Monitors

For events where cables would be unsightly or where speakers need to move around the stage freely, wireless confidence monitors offer flexibility. These use tablets or small wireless displays that the speaker can hold or place anywhere within range.

When to Use Confidence Monitors

Confidence monitors are ideal for:

Live Presentations

When speakers need to deliver prepared remarks but can't memorize the full script. This includes corporate keynotes, conference presentations, political events, and award ceremonies throughout Georgia.

Multi-Speaker Events

Conferences and conventions with multiple presenters benefit from confidence monitors at each podium position. Speakers can follow their specific sections without worrying about missing their cues or speaking time.

Live Broadcasts and Streaming

Television productions, livestreams, and virtual events often use confidence monitors to ensure speakers stay on script. Atlanta's growing film and production industry relies on confidence monitors for everything from news broadcasts to commercial shoots.

Religious Services

Churches and religious institutions across Georgia use confidence monitors for sermons, announcements, and special presentations. Many Savannah and Augusta churches have upgraded to professional confidence monitor setups.

Legal and Government Proceedings

Courtrooms, legislative sessions, and government meetings in Atlanta and throughout Georgia use confidence monitors for reading statements, official remarks, and procedural announcements.

Confidence Monitors vs. Traditional Teleprompters

Understanding the difference helps you choose the right setup:

Traditional Teleprompters (Presidential Style): Use reflective glass panels that display text visible only to the speaker. The audience sees nothing. Best for large audiences where you want the speaker to appear to be speaking naturally without reading.

Confidence Monitors: Display text visibly to everyone in the room. Best when you don't mind the audience seeing the script, or when the speaker prefers to glance at a screen rather than speak into a camera.

Many events use both—a presidential teleprompter setup for main stage presentations plus confidence monitors at each speaker position for easy reading.

Setting Up Confidence Monitors: Best Practices

Placement

Position confidence monitors directly in the speaker's natural line of sight. They shouldn't have to look up, down, or to the side dramatically to see the text. For podium setups, place the monitor at comfortable reading height.

Sizing

Text should be large enough to read comfortably without squinting. We typically recommend 24-32 inch monitors for stage setups, with larger displays for bigger venues or when speakers are far from the screen.

Brightness

Confidence monitors need to be bright enough to read in various lighting conditions. Stage lighting can create glare, so we position monitors at angles that minimize reflections.

Script Formatting

Text should be formatted for easy reading—typically 24-36 point font, high contrast, with adequate line spacing. Each screen should show only enough text for 2-3 minutes ahead to prevent information overload.

Operator Coordination

For live events, a teleprompter operator controls the scroll speed and advances slides. The operator should coordinate with the stage manager and have a clear view of the stage.

Common Confidence Monitor Issues and Solutions

Speaker Can't See the Monitor

This usually means improper placement or angle. We position monitors at the start of every setup and do a walkthrough with speakers before the event.

Text Too Small

We adjust font sizes based on venue size and monitor distance. For large venues like the Georgia World Congress Center, we may use multiple larger monitors.

Glare from Stage Lighting

We work with your AV team to position monitors at angles that avoid direct light reflection. In some cases, monitor hoods or anti-glare screens help.

Technical Failures

Backup equipment is essential. We carry spare monitors, cables, and processors to every event. For major corporate events in Atlanta, we often include redundant systems.
